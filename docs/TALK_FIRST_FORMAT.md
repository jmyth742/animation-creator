# The Talk Piece — a format built on what this pipeline does best

Measured across nineteen episodes, the instruments rank:

  1. **Dialogue close-ups** — the strongest shot in the toolkit. Lips sync,
     identity holds, 15s chains work. This is the show's engine.
  2. **Static two-shots** (from a generated plate) — two people genuinely
     share a frame. Good for the beat before/after words, not during.
  3. **Held wides** — geography, weather, punctuation. Reliable when static.
  4. **Locomotion** — the weakest axis. Characters walking read as drifting
     blobs at distance; motion is soupy up close. No setting fixes this;
     it is the model.

The conclusion the numbers keep pointing at: **stop asking for the weak
instrument.** A talk piece is a small story told almost entirely in
alternating close-ups — two people working through one thing in one place.

## Shape (3–5 minutes, one location)

    1. WIDE      the place, empty or with figures already settled   ~8s
    2. CLOSE     speaker A opens                                    2–3 lines
    3. CLOSE     speaker B answers                                  2–3 lines
    4. …alternate. Every cut back to a face is free and looks good.
    5. TWO-SHOT  one silent beat mid-piece — let them sit in it     ~7s
    6. CLOSE     the turn: someone says the thing they came to say
    7. WIDE      the place again, changed by what was said          ~8s

Movement, if any, is **arrival or departure only**, silent, one shot,
under 5 seconds, full-body plate — punctuation, never a scene.

## Why this reads as premium rather than cheap

Alternating-closes conversation is the grammar of My Dinner with Andre,
of Bergman, of half of Aaron Sorkin — and of prestige animation:
Kaguya's court scenes, Castlevania's council chapters. The audience reads
held faces as *intensity*, not economy. What reads as cheap is exactly
what the weak instrument produces: unconvincing walking.

## Writing rules (enforceable today)

- One place per piece. One subject per piece. Two speakers, one at a time.
- ≤39 words per line (S2V chain ceiling), 2–3 lines per close.
- Give the silent two-shot a real beat to hold (a look, a hand, weather).
- Recorded voice (⏺ in the composer) beats TTS for the turn — give the
  most important line a human take.
- The camera does the moving, not the characters: PTZ slow push-ins on
  the confession, pull-backs on the wides.
